How it works. Get your badge at the reception, then take missions (blue markers) or courier jobs from the job board in the lobby. Explore the 3 GeV ring (528 m) and the 1.5 GeV ring, walk over the bridges into the storage-ring tunnel area, visit the beamlines and the 300 m linac. Breaking interlocks, driving cars in the hall or running people over raises the ☢ safety alert – the safety officers will escort you to training. Find all the hidden golden photons ✦.

Visitable beamlines. All beamlines are in the hall; only CoSAXS (SAXS/WAXS) and DanMAX (powder diffraction and PDF) are open (★ on the map). In the hutch, take a calibrant (green table) or a sample (purple table). Then search the hutch: button 1 at the far end → button 2 by the door → leave → LOCK outside within 30 s. Opening the hutch cancels the search. In the cabin: 1 Measure (live hutch camera) · 2 Calibrate and save · 3 Reduce 2D → 1D (mask + geometry) · then CoSAXS: 4 Analyse (sphere fit, Debye structures) · DanMAX: 4 PDF (I(Q) → S(Q) → F(Q) → G(r), with background and Q range) and 5 Analyse in Q-space (fit Bragg peaks → lattice parameter and Scherrer size) and r-space (crystal-sphere fit → lattice parameter and particle size).

Around the hall. On your left as you come in, past CoSAXS: the exhibition “From MAX I to MAX IV”. Table tennis is in the open space between the future beamline and DanMAX.

Money. Good science pays: each clean dataset of a sample is worth an hour of beamtime (≈ kr 6 260, ALBA’s published rate of €544/h), and goals, analyses, missions and courier jobs pay kr 3 000–50 000. Accidents cost what they would in reality: a detector module burnt by the direct beam ≈ kr 350 000 (estimate; vendors don’t publish prices – until then mask the bad pixels), a forced interlock ≈ kr 100 000 of lost beamtime, an emergency-room visit kr 400 (Region Skåne). Bills can put you in debt (red).

🤖 Autonomous synthesis. In both hutches a synthesis robot makes gold nanoparticles; in the control cabin’s Autonomous synthesis tab you set a target, the beamline measures each sample and Bayesian optimisation picks the next recipe. The idea and method are from our paper: A. S. Anker et al., “Autonomous Synthesis of Nanoparticles with Target Scattering Patterns”, ACS Nano 2026, 20, 6767–6782, doi:10.1021/acsnano.5c15488 (the chemistry in the game is simulated).

Scattering patterns are calculated with DebyeCalculator 1.1.1 (grid pair sum, PR #44): F. L. Johansen, A. S. Anker, U. Friis-Jensen, E. B. Dam, K. M. Ø. Jensen, R. Selvan, “A GPU-Accelerated Open-Source Python Package for Calculating Powder Diffraction, Small-Angle-, and Total Scattering with the Debye Scattering Equation”, J. Open Source Softw. 2024, 9(94), 6024, doi:10.21105/joss.06024. The analysis tab’s literature example (Bi-oxido cluster growth, Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2021, e202103641) was measured elsewhere, not at CoSAXS; it comes from the course app saxs_analysis.

Layout is a playful, simplified version of the real site – beamline positions are approximate.

Software & data. 3D engine three.js (MIT licence). Scattering patterns precomputed with DebyeCalculator (Apache 2.0; please cite Johansen, Anker et al., J. Open Source Softw. 2024, 9(94), 6024, doi:10.21105/joss.06024) and ASE. X-ray form factors: Cromer–Mann coefficients, International Tables for Crystallography Vol. C. Detector specifications: DECTRIS data sheets. Repair costs are estimates; the beamtime rate is ALBA’s published rate.
